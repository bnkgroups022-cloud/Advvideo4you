Frequently asked questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The notebook uses Colab's free T4 GPU. Google decides GPU availability and session limits for free accounts, so you may occasionally have to wait or try again later.

Do I need an account?

You need a Google account to use Colab. This website has no accounts, no servers that receive your data and no tracking of its own.

Where does my photo go?

Your browser resizes it and saves it, together with your settings, into a launch file on your device. You upload that file to your own Colab session, where it becomes the first frame of the video. It is never sent to this website.

Which models does it use?

Video: Wan2.1 VACE-1.3B through the official Wan-Video/Wan2.1 repository (your photo is the first frame). Script: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, with curated templates as a fallback. Voice: Piper TTS. Music: an original synthesised loop.

How long is the video, and how long does it take?

The ad is 15 seconds. The AI motion is a clip of about 2 seconds (33 frames) that plays forward and backward in a loop with a slow camera drift, so it repeats; the voice, captions and music carry the ad. The first run also downloads about 22 GB of models, so it takes much longer. The target for later runs is 6–8 minutes, but that has not been measured on a real T4.

What if it runs out of memory?

The notebook retries with a shorter AI clip (25, then 17 frames). If the clip still cannot be made, it renders the ad with a slow push-in on your photo instead and tells you so.

Why do I upload a launch file in Colab?

Colab cannot receive settings through a link. So this page writes one file with your photo and choices, and the notebook reads it in its single upload prompt. If you skip it and upload only a photo, the notebook asks for the name, language and call to action on the spot.

Can I use the videos commercially?

Check the licences first. The English voice is public domain and the Bangla voice is CC BY-SA (credit required), but the Hindi voice is licensed for non-commercial use only. Wan2.1, Qwen and the Noto fonts allow commercial use. AI-written lines are not verified facts, so review the script before publishing.
